Why participate

Low-cost land is not the same as a low-cost project. The Academy helps nonprofit leaders decide whether, when and how real estate activity serves their mission, and how to carry a project from idea to stewardship responsibly.

Nonprofit infrastructure

Board authority, policies, controls, reserves and staffing that real estate activity demands.

Mission alignment

Test whether ownership, development, leasing or partnering truly advances documented community need.

Responsible acquisition

Due diligence before site control: title, taxes, condition, zoning and feasibility.

Sustainable delivery

Capital, team, fair housing and compliance practices that hold up after the deed is recorded.

Eight integrated modules, two half days

One illustrative parcel carries through every module, using the Shelby County Land Bank Nominal Consideration Conveyance (NCC) program as the core case study. The skills transfer to any nonprofit real estate activity.

Who are you building it for?

A healing-centered community development overlay, with fair housing woven into every decision from mission to stewardship. Each team builds a Commitment Statement one module at a time: the community served, who is at risk of being left out, and how they will prove access was fair.

  1. Who is here now? Residents, legacy families and the history of the block.
  2. Who has been left out or harmed? And how could a decision quietly exclude them again?
  3. Who will have access, and how will we prove it? Accessible design, open marketing, fair selection, accommodation and records.
CultureHonor the neighborhood's history, identity and assets.
ConnectionBuild relationships before the parcel request, and keep them through completion.
Power-shiftingShare decisions, contracts, ownership and wealth with the community.
HealingRepair, not repeat, past harm through stable, safe and accessible places.

The healing-centered lens draws on Enterprise Community Partners' Building to Heal framework. Fair housing content is educational, not legal advice, and does not replace the Shelby County Land Bank's required NCC Workshop Certification.

Know Before You Convey

Land Bank parcels often come out of delinquent tax proceedings and are conveyed as-is. Learn what to examine before you request a property, while you can still walk away.

Know the land. Understand the liability. Build the team. Test the feasibility. Then pursue the conveyance.

  • Quitclaim deedsWhat a deed conveyed without warranties does and does not give you, and why title review still matters.
  • The tax judgment versus other outstanding taxesWhy some taxes or charges may remain after conveyance, and how to check before you commit.
  • Title defectsGaps in the chain, liens, easements, heirs' interests and insurability questions to raise with a title professional.
  • Due diligence before site controlUnlike a contract with an inspection period, pre-application research happens up front, with limited access, real costs and no guarantee of conveyance.
  • Property feasibilityZoning, flood zone, survey, utilities and site condition, tested against your end use and budget.
  • Strategic partnershipsWhen to bring in counsel, surveyors, design professionals, lenders and development partners.

Educational content only, not legal, tax or title advice. Confirm current program rules with the Shelby County Land Bank and consult licensed Tennessee professionals.
